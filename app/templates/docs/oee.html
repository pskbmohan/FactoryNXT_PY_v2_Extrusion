Measure how well your machines are really performing — the gold standard for manufacturing productivity.

What is this?

OEE measures how well your extrusion presses are really performing compared to their theoretical maximum. It's a single percentage number built from three factors: Availability (is the machine running when it should be?), Performance (is it running at full speed?), and Quality (what fraction of output is good product?). World-class OEE is ≥ 85%.

How to use it

  1. Go to OEE from the sidebar — the dashboard loads with all machines.
  2. Select Machine and Date Range to focus your analysis.
  3. Read the three component scores to diagnose the bottleneck: Availability (downtime), Performance (speed), or Quality (scrap).
  4. Click "Downtime Breakdown" to see why availability was low — maintenance, die changes, material waits, etc.
  5. Click "Speed Loss" to see performance gap causes — slow cycles, minor stops, idling.
  6. Export to CSV for monthly management reporting or trend analysis.

Key fields explained

FieldWhat it means
Availability% of planned time the machine was running. Losses = downtime (breakdowns, changeovers, waits).
Performance% of theoretical speed the machine ran at. Losses = slow cycles, minor stops, idling.
Quality% of output that was good (not scrap or rework). Losses = defects, start-up waste.
OEEThe product of all three: Availability × Performance × Quality. The overall productivity score.
Trend (7d)How OEE has changed over the last 7 days — up (green) or down (red).

💡 Tips

  • World-class OEE is ≥ 85%. If you're below 70%, focus on Availability first — it's usually the biggest lever.
  • Downtime reason codes must be accurate — garbage in, garbage out. Train operators to use them consistently.
  • Track OEE daily, not monthly — you can't fix what you don't see in real time.
